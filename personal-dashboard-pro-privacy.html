Personal Dashboard Pro — Privacy Policy

What the Personal Dashboard Pro Chrome extension does, and does not do, with your data.

Draft version — last updated 10 September 2026. Not yet reviewed by a solicitor. This policy covers the Personal Dashboard Pro Chrome extension only. For this website, the free Budget Tracker and the free Personal Dashboard extension, see the site-wide privacy policy.

This page is a draft, not legal advice. It is written to be cautious and accurate about what the extension actually does, and every statement below was checked against the extension's source code — but it has not been reviewed by a qualified solicitor.

The short version

  • There is no account and no sign-up. No Simple Money Tools server holds your data, because no such server exists.
  • Everything the extension stores — settings, bookmarks, watchlist, and any integration credential — is held in your browser's own extension storage, on your device.
  • Both integrations are optional, off until you connect them, and strictly read-only. The extension cannot place a trade, move money, or post to Strava.
  • No advertising, no selling of data, and no analytics or tracking of any kind inside the extension.
  • You can disconnect an integration, or delete every trace of local data, at any time — free, on every tier.

No account, no server

Personal Dashboard Pro has no user accounts, no sign-up, no login and no server-side user database. Simple Money Tools does not operate a backend that receives or stores your dashboard data, your portfolio figures, your activity data, or a profile of you — there is no such system for it to be stored in.

All of the extension's data lives in Chrome's local extension storage on the device you installed it on. It is not synced to your Google account, not copied to your other devices, and not transmitted to Simple Money Tools.

Trading 212 (optional, read-only)

The Trading 212 integration is off until you deliberately connect it, and works only with an API key that you generate yourself inside your own Trading 212 account.

  • Where the key is stored: on your device only, in the browser's local extension storage. It is never synced across devices, never sent to Simple Money Tools, and never included in an exported settings file — the export is built from an explicit allow-list of safe fields, so a credential cannot end up in it by accident.
  • What it is used for: exactly two read-only requests — your account summary, and your open positions. That is the entire surface the extension touches.
  • What it cannot do: the extension cannot place trades, cancel or modify orders, deposit, withdraw, or change anything about your account. No such capability exists anywhere in its code, and no write endpoint is ever called.
  • Where the request goes: directly from your browser to Trading 212's own API. It does not pass through the Simple Money Tools proxy described below, and no third party sits in between.
  • Account types: Trading 212's API covers Invest and Stocks ISA accounts. SIPP and CFD accounts are not exposed by that API at all, and are not supported.

Fetched portfolio data is cached locally for a short period so the dashboard does not re-request it on every new tab. That cache sits on your device, is never synced, and can be cleared on its own from Settings → Privacy.

Strava (optional, read-only)

The Strava integration is also off until you connect it, and is authorised through Strava's own sign-in page using OAuth.

  • Your password: you enter it on Strava's own website, never in the extension. The extension never sees it and never stores it.
  • Scope: read-only access to your activities and profile statistics. No write scope is ever requested, and no write endpoint appears anywhere in the code — the extension cannot post, edit or delete an activity, or change anything on your Strava account.
  • Tokens: the resulting access and refresh tokens are stored the same way as the Trading 212 key — locally only, never synced, never exported.
  • Where the request goes: directly from your browser to Strava's own API. It does not pass through the Simple Money Tools proxy.

Figures the extension works out for itself are not Strava's figures. The fitness estimate, the activity streak, and the weekly trend rows — Activities, Activity time, Swim, Bike and Run — are all calculated by Personal Dashboard Pro from the activity data it reads. They are the extension's own calculations, are labelled as such wherever they appear, and are not official Strava metrics. Please do not read them as Strava's own numbers, and do not treat any of them as medical, health or fitness advice.

The Cloudflare Worker proxy

Some of the dashboard's data comes from third-party providers that require an API key. Shipping those keys inside the extension would publish them to anyone who unzipped it, so instead the extension calls a small Cloudflare Worker operated by Simple Money Tools, and the Worker adds the key server-side before forwarding the request.

What that means in practice: for these requests the provider sees a request arriving from the proxy rather than from your browser, so your IP address is not exposed to them. The proxy itself has no user accounts, no login and no per-user state — it cannot tell one user's request from another's, because it is never given anything that would identify you.

What passes through the proxy:

  • Weather: a coarse, rounded location and your unit preference. Your coordinates are rounded to two decimal places — roughly a kilometre — in your browser, before the request is sent, so a precise location never leaves your device. Nothing else about you is included.
  • Stock and ETF prices: the ticker symbol being looked up or shown on your watchlist. Not who is asking, and not your holdings.
  • Background photos: a single topic keyword, such as "mountains". Nothing else.

What does not pass through the proxy: your Trading 212 data, your Strava data, any credential or token, your bookmarks, your browsing history, your name, or your email address. None of these are ever sent to it.

The Worker caches provider responses at its edge so that repeat requests share one upstream call. It caches the response, not the requester.

Other connections the extension makes

For completeness: these requests go directly from your browser rather than through the proxy, which means the provider concerned can see your IP address in the ordinary way that any website you visit can.

  • Cryptocurrency prices are fetched directly from CoinGecko's free public API. It needs no key, so there is nothing for the proxy to protect.
  • Background photo images are loaded from the photo provider's own image servers, once the proxy has chosen which photo to show.
  • Bookmark icons are fetched from Google's public favicon service, which is given the address of the bookmarked site in order to return its icon.
  • Trading 212 and Strava, as described above — if, and only if, you have connected them.

Background photos

Background photos come from Unsplash, with Pexels as a fallback when Unsplash's shared hourly quota is exhausted. Each photo is credited on the dashboard to its photographer and its source. Neither provider is told anything about you — only the topic keyword.

On the free tier the extension fetches up to five new photos per day, then reshuffles recent ones rather than requesting more; Pro removes that cap. This is a cost control on a shared API quota, not a data-collection mechanism — the count is simply a number in your own local storage.

Payment

Personal Dashboard Pro is a one-off £5 purchase, handled by ExtensionPay on its own hosted checkout. Card details are entered on ExtensionPay's page and never touch the extension or Simple Money Tools — we never see, receive or store them. The extension holds an anonymous licence key locally and uses it only to ask ExtensionPay whether this installation has been paid for. Your use of that checkout is subject to ExtensionPay's own privacy policy.

Disconnecting and deleting your data

Either integration can be disconnected at any time from Settings → Privacy. Disconnecting removes that integration's stored credential from your device.

The same panel has a one-click "Delete all local Personal Dashboard Pro data" control, which permanently removes every connected account, every stored credential and every cached record from your device. It asks you to confirm first, and it is free for every user — it is deliberately not a Pro feature, because putting a paywall in front of someone deleting their own data would be indefensible.

Uninstalling the extension also removes its local storage, as Chrome does for any extension.

What this extension does not do

  • No advertising, and no ad networks.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of personal data. There is nothing collected to sell.
  • No third-party analytics, telemetry, tracking pixels or usage reporting of any kind. There is no analytics code in the extension.
  • No reading of your browsing history, and no content scripts running on the pages you visit. The only content script runs on ExtensionPay's own checkout page, to confirm a completed purchase.
  • No fabricated figures. A metric that is genuinely unavailable is shown as unavailable, and one you have chosen to hide is shown as hidden — the two are always distinguished, never guessed at.

Cookies

The extension sets no cookies of its own, and has no cookie banner or consent flow, because it has nothing to ask consent for. Third-party sites you open from it, including ExtensionPay's checkout, are governed by their own policies.

Children

Personal Dashboard Pro is not directed at children, and connecting a Trading 212 or Strava account requires an account with those providers under their own terms.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you have rights to access, correct and delete personal data held about you. Because Personal Dashboard Pro holds no personal data on any server — everything is on your own device, under your own control — there is in practice nothing held about you to request, and deletion is something you can carry out yourself at any time using the control described above. If you believe that is not the case in your situation, please get in touch using the address below.

Changes to this policy

If the extension's data handling changes, this page will be updated before that change ships, not after. The "last updated" date at the top of this page reflects the most recent revision.
